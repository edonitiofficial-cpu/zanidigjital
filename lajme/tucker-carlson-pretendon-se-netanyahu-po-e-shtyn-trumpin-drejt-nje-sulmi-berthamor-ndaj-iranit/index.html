Tucker Carlson pretendon se Netanyahu po e shtyn Trumpin drejt një sulmi bërthamor ndaj Iranit

Ndaj

Nën presionin e kryeministrit izraelit Benjamin Netanyahu, presidenti amerikan Donald Trump mund të ndërmarrë sulme bërthamore ndaj Iranit këtë javë, ka paralajmëruar komentatori konservator Tucker Carlson.

Carlson, dikur mik dhe tani kritik i Trumpit, deklaroi të dielën në platformën X se Netanyahu i ka bërë presion Trumpit që të rifillojë luftën amerikano-izraelite kundër Iranit.

Ai pretendoi se ka gjasa që Trumpi të sulmojë objektin nëntokësor iranian në "Malin Pickaxe" (Pickaxe Mountain), pranë Natanzit, shkruajnë mediat e huaja, përcjell

Sipas Carlson, pas rezultateve të zgjedhjeve të javës së kaluar, Netanyahu e ka kuptuar se mbijetesa e tij politike varet nga rifillimi i luftës.

Ai pohoi se Netanyahu nuk mund të formojë qeveri nëse Trumpi nuk ndërhyn drejtpërdrejt në favor të tij.

Edhe pse Shtetet e Bashkuara kanë sulmuar më parë objektet bërthamore iraniane në Natanz, Fordow dhe Isfahan, ato ende nuk e kanë vënë në shënjestër "Malin Pickaxe".

Under pressure from Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu, the Trump administration may be within days of launching a nuclear strike on Iran. The target would be Pickax Mountain in central Iran, where Israeli intelligence claims the Iranian government has restarted its nuclear enrichment program. No conventional weapon in the American arsenal can penetrate 300 feet of solid granite to destroy the tunnels where enrichment is supposedly taking place. The only military option is a nuclear strike. Netanyahu has been pushing hard for one. “Bibi calls Trump about it every day,” says an informed observer.Netanyahu is cornered and desperate. After last week’s election results, he can’t form a government unless Trump intervenes directly in Israeli politics and brokers a deal with other parties that gives Netanyahu 61 seats in the Knesset. Otherwise, he is out as prime minister and on his way to ignominy, investigations and possibly jail. Netanyahu must stay close to Trump. He needs this war.To that end, he’s applied maximum pressure to his closest allies in the administration, including Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth and CENTCOM commander Brad Cooper. Both oppose a negotiated settlement to the war they’ve so grievously mismanaged; both are pushing to restart major combat operations. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and CIA director John Ratcliffe are also following Netanyahu’s lead, as are leaders of the Democratic Party. In a recent meeting on the subject, former president Barack Obama said he opposes the current Iran peace deal being drafted in Doha.The rest of America’s longtime allies in the Middle East are terrified by the prospect of more war. On September 27th, Netanyahu flew secretly to Abu Dhabi to enlist the UAE in his lobbying effort for a final attack on Iran. The Emiratis refused. After eight months of conflict, Gulf economies are teetering. So are global energy and fertilizer markets. They won’t recover soon. Globally, there are a total of about 850 supertankers used to transport oil from producers to the rest of the world. In October alone, 11 of them were badly damaged in Hormuz. At this pace, a global depression is imminent. A nuclear strike would radically accelerate the destruction. Fallout from a ground explosion at Pickax Mountain would contaminate the Persian Gulf, poisoning the water supply for 100 million people.And those are just some of the immediate consequences for the region. The rest of the world is every bit as exposed. No country other than the United States has ever used a nuclear weapon in war. Some would like to. An American nuclear attack on Iran would set a new precedent. The Russians have already told at least two other governments that an American nuclear strike on Iran would free them to use nuclear weapons against Ukraine. Pakistan and India would be tempted to do the same against each other, as would North Korea against Seoul. After the first nuclear strike, World War Three is inevitable. That war would be short and final.We’ve been sleepwalking toward this moment for months. Since February, the administration has quietly rewritten America’s nuclear doctrine to make first use of so-called “battlefield nukes” easier and therefore more likely. No one in Washington in either party seems bothered by this. Now we’re on the brink of it.Close observers say Trump is likely to decide by next weekend whether or not to use nuclear weapons. That would be November 7, six days from now. To his credit, he’s hesitating. At the moment, Trump is listening to his vice president, JD Vance, and to his envoy and friend Steve Witkoff, both of whom argue a deal with Iran can be made.They’re right, though it won’t be simple. The Iranians are famously frustrating to negotiate with under any circumstances, and elements of that government have become more radical since Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and his family were killed in February. After decades of isolation, even relatively moderate Iranian leaders seem confused by how decisions are made in the West. (There is a widespread belief in Tehran, for example, that the United States government controls Israel.) But overall, people who have spoken to decision-makers in Iran say there is consensus there that more war is bad for everyone and a deal is worth making.One group that hasn’t been consulted so far is the global population — the hundreds of millions of people who will be killed along with their children if Trump is pushed by Netanyahu into launching a nuclear strike next week. Now would be the time for them to speak up.

Ky objekt ndodhet thellë brenda një mali — më thellë se objekti në Fordow — dhe është jashtë rrezes së veprimit të shumicës së armëve konvencionale.

Trumpi më herët ka lënë të kuptohet se sulmet ndaj këtij vendndodhjeje janë një mundësi reale.

Por analistët thonë se mbi këtë objekt ndodhen të paktën 100 metra shkëmb mali.

Pavarësisht pretendimit të Carlsonit, Trumpi më parë e ka përjashtuar mundësinë e përdorimit të armëve bërthamore kundër Iranit.

Në vend të kësaj, ai ka sugjeruar përdorimin e "Nënës së të Gjitha Bombave".

"Nuk do të përdorja armë bërthamore. Nuk kemi pse. Ne jemi fuqia më e madhe bërthamore në botë", tha Trump në fillim të këtij muaji.

Carlson pretendoi se aleatët e Netanyahut brenda administratës, si Sekretari i Mbrojtjes, Pete Hegseth, si dhe komandanti i Komandës Qendrore, Admirali Brad Cooper, po lobojnë në favor të tij; gjithashtu, Sekretari i Shtetit Marco Rubio thuhet se e mbështet idenë e sulmeve bërthamore ndaj Iranit.

"Asnjë armë konvencionale në arsenalin amerikan nuk mund të depërtojë nëpër rreth 90 metra granit të fortë për të shkatërruar tunelet ku, siç pretendohet, po kryhet pasurimi. I vetmi opsion ushtarak është një sulm bërthamor. Netanyahu ka bërë presion të fortë për këtë. 'Bibi e telefonon Trumpin çdo ditë për këtë çështje,'thotë një vëzhgues i mirinformuar", u shpreh Carlson.
